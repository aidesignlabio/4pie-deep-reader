# Domain depth modules

Use these questions to create detail with decision value. Answer only questions supported by the available data, but cover at least four subquestions in each core domain.

## Career and status

1. What work function is strongest: creating, researching, teaching, advising, selling, operating, managing, or leading?
2. Is progress driven by specialist skill, public visibility, institutional rank, ownership, or network access?
3. Which work environment improves performance, and which repeatedly produces failure?
4. Does the person build one long ladder, maintain two tracks, or periodically reset direction?
5. What causes stagnation: weak skill, wrong role, authority conflict, inconsistent output, or unstable environment?
6. How do early career, the current major period, and later authority differ?
7. Which three career families rank highest, and which attractive option should be rejected?

## Wealth

1. Where does earned income come from?
2. Is income stable, project-based, cyclical, shared, or platform-dependent?
3. Is the main financial weakness low earnings, volatility, hidden costs, fixed expenses, family absorption, or shared money?
4. What converts earnings into retained wealth?
5. What types of debt, investment, or joint finance carry the highest downside?
6. When do income growth and asset consolidation occur, and are they the same period?
7. Separate cash flow, savings, property, investment, and partner/family money.

## Relationship and marriage

1. What creates attraction, and what creates trust?
2. What partner qualities are repeatedly selected?
3. Is the main difficulty partner selection, delayed disclosure, distance, work conflict, family, money, intimacy, or commitment timing?
4. Distinguish meeting, attraction, exclusivity, shared routine, cohabitation, marriage, and shared assets.
5. What typically breaks the relationship, and what allows repair?
6. Does relationship improve, constrain, or redirect career and location?
7. Which windows favor meeting, testing, formalizing, or ending?

## Home, family, relocation, and property

1. How strongly does the original family shape early decisions?
2. Is adult development helped by staying, leaving, or alternating bases?
3. What triggers relocation: study, career, relationship, family duty, or property?
4. Is property primarily security, investment, responsibility, or financial pressure?
5. Which family obligations can interrupt career or cash flow?
6. When is independent living or property consolidation most likely?

## Learning, network, reputation, and stress

Use these only when they change a core outcome:

- learning style, qualifications, overseas education, and examination periods;
- friends versus useful professional networks, collaborator selection, and group conflict;
- public reputation, controversy, audience, and authority;
- workload pattern, recovery conditions, and non-diagnostic stress risks.

## Detail quality test

A paragraph adds depth only if it does at least one of the following:

- separates two outcomes that a broad summary would confuse;
- ranks plausible alternatives;
- identifies a different mechanism at a different life stage;
- narrows an event family or activation period;
- states a downside that changes a real decision;
- gives a falsifiable result.

Delete paragraphs that merely repeat a trait, restate advice, translate technical terms without changing the ruling, or add generic encouragement.
