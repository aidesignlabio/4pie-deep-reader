# Prompt set

## A. Native outcome judgment

Analyze one school only from verified data. Do not describe a personality unless it directly changes an outcome.

Select the three life outcomes this school can judge most strongly. For each, rank a primary path, secondary path, and rejected path. Explain why the primary path wins, what produces its favorable and unfavorable realization, which natal facts permit it, and which timing layers activate it. State the observable result that would invalidate the ruling.

Assign a stable `claim_id`. Cite exact source fact IDs or evidence paths. Record the question, time layer, method, calculation status, and applicability. Never replace evidence with a configuration summary.

Do not return an unranked list. Do not call every difficulty a lesson. Do not infer an event from one symbol.

For every selected domain, answer the applicable subquestions from `depth-modules.md`. Distinguish ability, desire, environment, repeated outcome, and timing. Do not collapse them into a single story.

## B. Outcome challenge

Reject any judgment that lacks a ranked result, meaningful downside, alternative path, deciding condition, timing permission, failure rule, or traceable source facts. Reject generic statements and fake numerical probability. Downgrade unstable divisional houses, unverified transformations, and unresolved Bazi strength-dependent claims.

## C. Cross-school fate adjudication

Compare approved native outcomes answering the same question and time layer. Select a primary, secondary, and rejected path. Preserve conflicts when no path wins. State the favorable branch, unfavorable branch, deciding choices or external conditions, and the periods when the branch becomes active.

Do not vote by school count. Do not merge incompatible outcomes into a neutral sentence.

For every candidate claim, construct a four-school position map using `support`, `refine`, `limit`, `oppose`, `not_comparable`, `not_applicable`, or `insufficient`. `Oppose` requires the same question, same time layer, and mutually incompatible outcomes. Retain each school's native reasoning and evidence paths.

Assign `high_consensus`, `moderate_consensus`, `single_school_strong_signal`, `conflict`, or `insufficient` according to `transparency-model.md`. Explain the tier and keep event certainty separate. Never turn school count into probability.

Before the domain summary, adjudicate its subquestions separately. A detailed domain must identify at least one internal fork: two plausible life versions, the evidence selecting between them, and the condition that can move the person from one branch to the other.

## T. Timing windows

Build the chain `natal permission -> background period -> sensitivity window -> transition date -> event carrier -> event family`. Rank no more than five windows. Give one primary domain and at most two secondary domains. List no more than three event families. State the real-world carrier, contrary signal, and result that would count as failure. Every exact cycle-change date must be labelled `not_event_date: true`. A failed external prediction cannot be renamed internal preparation.

Then create annual rulings for every requested year. Explain continuity: what enters the year, what becomes decidable, what can materialize, and what remains unfinished. Do not give every year the same themes.

## D. Fortune report

Write for a reader with no astrology knowledge. Use this order: one-page consequential summary; four short native-school readings; cross-school adjudication by domain; integrated timeline; decision table; technical appendix. Name the four schools and explain their roles in this run.

Every section must say what is most likely, what the better version looks like, what the worse version looks like, what changes the result, when it activates, and what would prove it wrong. After every major conclusion, include a compact evidence box showing four school positions, consensus tier, event certainty when relevant, and one unresolved or validation question. Put calculation detail in the appendix.

Keep chart judgment, real-life projection, action advice, and validation condition separate. Never describe a projection as known biography, and never present advice as evidence.

Ban vague substitutes for outcomes: `找到自己的方法`, `重新定義身份`, `資源配置`, `承載`, `對齊`, `內在轉變`, `模型`, `系統`, `邊界課題`, and `成長機會`.

Use a layered but natural reader structure:

1. State the concrete ruling in ordinary language.
2. Show the two or three life versions that were considered and why one wins.
3. Explain how the outcome changes from early life to the current/next major period.
4. Give the favorable and unfavorable realization as recognizable situations.
5. Add timing only after the natal mechanism is clear.

Do not print the same six labels in every section. The logical functions must be present, but prose and section design should vary. Technical vocabulary may appear in a short “why this judgment” paragraph or the appendix; it must never be required to understand the ruling.

## E. Transparency and redundancy audit

For every major conclusion, verify that a reader can identify which schools support, refine, limit or oppose it; what source facts were used; why the selected version defeated alternatives; and what would lower confidence. Rewrite any conclusion whose reasoning is hidden behind fluent prose.

Read the assembled report as a whole. Remove repeated mechanisms, advice and domain conclusions. Keep one canonical explanation of a global mechanism and show only its domain-specific consequences elsewhere.
